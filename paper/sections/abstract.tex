\begin{abstract}
Adapting a Text-to-SQL benchmark to a new database requires grounding its query structures in a different schema and data. The resulting workload must also support meaningful model comparisons. We present Schema2Bench, an evidence-grounded method for cross-domain benchmark migration. It combines schema- and value-grounded SQL construction, execution checks, question generation and quota-constrained selection. A tight perturbation bound limits selection-induced changes in source fidelity, while a structural coverage bound identifies candidate shortages. Evaluation distinguishes source fidelity, the preservation of source model behavior, from target alignment, the reproduction of accuracy differences on target reference tasks. The main comparison uses Spider as the source and eight BIRD databases as targets, with five constructions, ten models and 2,000 tasks per method. Under execution scoring and the declared weights, deterministic refill reduces target-gap error from 0.225 to 0.169 relative to templates and has the lowest source-fidelity error; Ring has lower target gap and mean selection regret. Most gap improvement comes from construction. Same-SQL rewriting improves alignment but reduces fidelity; semantic sensitivity leaves both directions unresolved. Earlier experiments on seven additional databases support repair and bounded selection. Studies on SynSQL-2.5M, TACO and BEAVER examine construction choices and transfer, with context-dependent results.
\end{abstract}
