\begin{abstract}
Adapting a Text-to-SQL benchmark to a new database requires grounding its query structures in a different schema and data. The resulting workload must also support meaningful model comparisons. We present Schema2Bench, an evidence-grounded method for cross-domain benchmark migration. It combines schema- and value-grounded SQL construction, execution checks, question generation and quota-constrained selection. A tight perturbation bound limits selection-induced changes in source fidelity, while a structural coverage bound identifies candidate shortages. Evaluation distinguishes source fidelity, the preservation of model behavior on the source benchmark, from target alignment, the reproduction of accuracy differences on target reference tasks. The main comparison uses Spider as the source and eight databases from BIRD as targets, with five constructions, ten models and 2,000 tasks per method. Under execution-based scoring and the declared weights, deterministic refill reduces target-gap error from 0.225 to 0.169 relative to templates and has the lowest source-fidelity error. Ring-style construction gives lower target gap and mean selection regret. Most gap improvement comes from construction. Rewriting questions with SQL fixed improves target alignment but reduces source fidelity; semantic sensitivity leaves these directions unresolved. Additional studies on SynSQL-2.5M, TACO and BEAVER examine construction choices and transfer, with context-dependent results.
\end{abstract}
