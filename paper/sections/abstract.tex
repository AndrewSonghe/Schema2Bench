\begin{abstract}
Evaluating Text-to-SQL systems on a new database requires a workload grounded in its schema and data. Existing benchmarks offer reusable query structures, but adapting them can change the differences measured between models. We present Schema2Bench, a framework for constructing migrated workloads and evaluating the model comparisons they preserve. It separates source fidelity, the preservation of model behavior on the source benchmark, from target alignment, the reproduction of accuracy differences on target reference tasks. Schema2Bench combines schema- and value-grounded SQL construction, question realization and quota-constrained selection. A tight bound controls selection-induced changes in source fidelity; a structural certificate identifies coverage deficits in the candidate pool. We compare five constructions across eight BIRD databases and ten models, using 2,000 tasks per method and 906 target references. Under execution-refined scoring and the declared weights, deterministic refill reduces target-gap error from 0.225 to 0.169 relative to template construction and has the lowest source-fidelity error. Ring-style construction gives lower target gap and mean model-selection regret. Most target-gap gain precedes selection. Question rewriting with SQL fixed further reduces gap but increases source-fidelity error under execution scoring; joint semantic-risk analysis leaves these directions unresolved. External pilots show context-dependent tradeoffs. The results support choosing benchmark construction according to its intended evaluation use.
\end{abstract}
